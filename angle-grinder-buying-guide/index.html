How to choose the right angle grinder

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The best cordless angle grinder for you comes down to the battery platform you already own, the kind of work you’ll do (light cutting vs. heavy grinding), and whether you value a compact lightweight tool or one with more sustained power. Stick with a brushless motor, an electronic brake, and a paddle switch for safety, and you’ll get a capable workhorse that’s freed from the cord.

What to look for in a cordless angle grinder in 2026

Battery technology has matured, so today’s cordless grinders can rival corded models for many tasks. The first decision is voltage: most serious grinders run on a high-voltage platform (often 18 V or 20 V max), delivering enough torque for cutting rebar or grinding down welds. You’ll also see compact models on lower voltage lines, great for light cutting but not sustained heavy work. Beyond voltage, look for a brushless motor, which uses electronics to adjust power on the fly, generates less heat, and extends both runtime and motor life. A paddle switch with a lock-off button is the safest trigger style because it requires you to keep holding it, let go and the disc stops. An electronic brake is a real plus: it stops the disc within seconds of releasing the trigger, reducing the risk of injury when you set the tool down.

Battery platform and power: how voltage and amp-hours matter

Voltage determines the ceiling of power. A high-voltage grinder can maintain speed under load, which matters when you’re bearing down on a cut. Amp-hours (Ah) tell you how long a single charge lasts. For occasional use, a standard battery will do; for all-day work, high-capacity packs (often in the 5 Ah or higher range) are worth the extra weight. But battery compatibility is the real tiebreaker. If you already own drills or impact drivers from a major brand, sticking with that battery system saves you the cost of new batteries and chargers. Each brand’s high-voltage batteries are typically interchangeable within their lineup, so you can grab a bigger battery from another tool in the family. Just don’t assume cross-brand compatibility, they’re almost never interchangeable.

Disc size and speed: 4-1/2-inch vs. larger grinders

The vast majority of cordless angle grinders take a disc size that is very popular and widely available. These smaller discs cut through steel, tile, and thin metal quickly, and the grinder body stays compact and light. For heavier tasks like cutting thick rebar or deep notches in stone, a grinder that accepts a slightly larger disc can be more productive. Speed is typically governed around a specific no-load RPM. With a brushless motor, most models offer variable speed or a built-in governor that keeps RPM steady as you load the disc. A variable-speed trigger or dial is handy if you switch between cutting and grinding or work on different materials.

Safety features: kickback protection, paddle switches, and guards

Angle grinders are one of the most dangerous handheld tools if not used properly. A paddle switch is safer than a slide switch because it forces you to keep your hand on the tool, and it shuts off power the moment you release it. Many modern cordless models also have a kickback protection circuit that shuts the motor off if the disc binds or stalls. Always use the guard that comes with the grinder, it can be positioned to deflect sparks away from your face and body. A two-position or click-on guard makes this easy without removing the disc. Finally, a spindle lock or brake helps you change discs quickly and safely without needing an extra wrench.

Brushless vs. brushed motors: what’s the difference?

A brushed motor has physical carbon brushes that wear out and need replacement; a brushless motor uses electronic controllers to deliver power. Brushless motors are more efficient, meaning longer runtime per charge and more torque per watt. They also tend to run cooler, so you can work longer without the tool overheating. Most cordless angle grinders sold today are brushless, especially in the mid-range and high-end categories. A brushed grinder might save you some money up front, but it will be less powerful, heavier, and likely die sooner. If you plan to use the grinder regularly, a brushless model is worth the premium.

Top brands to consider: which ecosystem fits your tools?

The biggest names in cordless angle grinders remain Milwaukee, DeWalt, Makita, and Bosch. Each offers a range of grinders on their proprietary battery platforms. Milwaukee’s high-performance line and DeWalt’s FlexVolt system can deliver near-corded power for heavy grinding. Makita’s 18 V LXT line has a deep lineup with excellent ergonomics, while Bosch’s 18 V line leans toward professional tradespeople who value low vibration. For lighter or budget-friendly use, Ryobi and Craftsman offer grinders that are affordable and share batteries with a wide range of home tools. If you’re a weekend warrior, these will serve you well for cutting bolts, stripping rust, or sharpening blades. In any case, read recent owner reviews on reliability and warranty service, some brands have longer track records with grinders than others.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a cordless angle grinder for heavy metal cutting like thick rebar or steel plate?

Yes, if you choose a high-voltage brushless model with a high-capacity battery. For continuous heavy cuts, you may need multiple batteries to get through the job, but the tool will handle it, just don’t expect it to be as fast as a corded grinder on the thickest material.

How long does a battery last on a cordless angle grinder?

Run time varies hugely based on the battery capacity (amp-hours), the voltage, and how hard you push the tool. A typical 5 Ah battery can give anywhere from ten to twenty minutes of continuous heavy grinding, or much longer for light cutting. Always bring at least two batteries for a full day of work.

Are cordless angle grinders as powerful as corded ones?

Modern high-voltage brushless grinders are close enough for most jobs, but a corded grinder still has an edge for sustained heavy grinding or cutting thick steel without bogging down. For general cutting, grinding, and surface prep, cordless is plenty powerful and much more convenient.

What is the best voltage for an angle grinder?

For serious use, look for a grinder that operates on a high-voltage platform (often 18 V or 20 V max from your brand). These models deliver enough torque for demanding work. Lower voltage grinders are fine for light-duty tasks but will struggle with heavy cutting or grinding.

Do all cordless angle grinders use the same batteries?

No, batteries are almost always specific to a brand and often to a voltage platform within that brand. However, many major brands have a single battery system that works across their full tool lineup (e.g., Milwaukee M18, DeWalt 20 V MAX, Makita 18 V LXT). You cannot use a battery from one brand in another brand’s tool without an adapter.

Paddle switch vs. slide switch, which is safer?

A paddle switch is safer because you must hold it constantly, and it shuts off instantly if you lose grip. Slide switches stay on until you slide them off, which is more convenient for prolonged grinding but increases risk if the tool is dropped or jerked. For most people, a paddle switch is the recommended choice.

In shortChoosing a cordless angle grinder in 2026 is mostly about picking the right battery ecosystem and deciding how much power you truly need. A brushless motor, paddle switch, and electronic brake are non-negotiable for safety and longevity. Don’t overspend on a high-capacity kit if you only cut the occasional bolt, but if you’re grinding welds or cutting thick metal, invest in a high-voltage model with at least one large battery. With a solid tool and good discs, a cordless grinder becomes one of the most versatile tools in your shop.
