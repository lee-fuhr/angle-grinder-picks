Angle grinder questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Cordless angle grinders offer real freedom of movement, but you’ll want to weigh battery voltage, motor type, and safety features before buying. For most DIYers and pros, a brushless 18V or 20V max model with a paddle switch and decent battery capacity delivers the best balance of power and runtime. The key is matching the tool to your most common cutting or grinding task, a 4-½ inch disc size handles the vast majority of jobs without being overly heavy.

What should I look for in a cordless angle grinder?

Start with the battery platform you already own, sticking with one brand’s system saves money and ensures you have spares. Next, consider motor type: brushless motors run cooler, last longer, and squeeze more runtime from each charge, though they cost more upfront. Voltage matters, but it’s not the whole story: a well-designed brushless 18V grinder can outwork an older brushed 20V model. Ergonomics and safety are often overlooked. A tool with a paddle switch (you must keep it pressed to run) is safer than a slide switch because it stops immediately if you lose your grip. Also check for a vibration-reducing handle and tool-free guard adjustment, small details that make a big difference during extended use. Finally, look at the grinder’s weight and balance. Heavier models with larger batteries can be tiring overhead, so read user feedback about real-world handling. Most 4-½ inch cordless grinders weigh in a similar range, but balance differs by brand.

How does battery voltage affect performance?

Higher voltage generally means more power and faster material removal, but it also adds weight and cost. Cordless angle grinders commonly run on 18V or 20V max (virtually the same nominal voltage) or 36V/40V max systems. The 18V class is the sweet spot for most tasks, cutting rebar, grinding welds, or slitting thin metal, especially with a quality brushless motor. Dual-battery 36V grinders (two 18V packs in series) can rival corded models for heavy stock removal, but they are bulkier and pricier. If your work involves continuous use of thick metal or concrete, the extra voltage may be worth the trade-off. For intermittent jobs or lighter materials, a standard 18V tool with a high-capacity battery is plenty. Battery amp-hour (Ah) rating also matters: a 5Ah battery provides roughly 25% more runtime than a 4Ah at the same voltage, though the difference varies with load. Always check which battery size is included with the tool and whether it charges quickly.

Are cordless angle grinders as powerful as corded?

In short: not quite, but they’re close enough for most work. A high-end brushless cordless grinder on a full charge delivers power comparable to a corded model in the 6-8 amp range. That’s sufficient for cutting rebar, grinding down welds, and slicing through most metal up to ¼ inch thick. For sustained heavy grinding or thick concrete cutting, a corded unit still has the edge because it never loses power as the battery drains. However, the cordless advantage in mobility often outweighs the modest power gap. You can climb a ladder, work in a tight corner, or take the tool to the material instead of bringing the material to the outlet. Many pros now own both: a cordless grinder for quick cuts and a corded one for long, demanding jobs. One honest note: as the battery depletes, power tapers noticeably. Some premium grinders have a consistent power curve (they maintain full output until the battery is nearly dead), but most budget models slow down earlier. User reviews are the best way to gauge real-world consistency.

What’s the difference between brushless and brushed motors?

Brushless motors use electronic commutation instead of carbon brushes, which means less friction, less heat, and longer life. They also deliver more torque per watt, so the tool can be smaller and lighter while producing equal or greater power. Virtually every top-tier cordless grinder now uses a brushless motor, and the price difference over brushed models has shrunk. Brushed motors are older technology but still common on entry-level grinders. They are cheaper to manufacture and repair, you can often replace brushes easily. The trade-offs: they run hotter, waste more battery energy as heat, and typically have a shorter overall lifespan. For occasional home use, a brushed grinder may be perfectly adequate and more budget-friendly. If you plan to use the grinder regularly or for demanding tasks, the extra cost of brushless is almost always justified by better runtime and fewer motor failures. Check the warranty: many brushless models carry multi-year coverage, reflecting their reliability.

How do I choose the right disc size?

The vast majority of buyers will be best served by a 4-½ inch disc size. It’s the standard for cordless grinders, offering the best balance of cutting depth, maneuverability, and disc availability. You can cut thin metal, rebar, tile, and masonry, as well as grind and sand. Discs are cheap and sold everywhere. Larger discs (5 or 6 inches) allow deeper cuts, useful for thicker pipe or concrete, but they require more power and bigger batteries. A 6-inch grinder on an 18V battery may bog down easily unless it’s a powerful brushless model. Smaller discs (3 or 3-½ inches) are great for tight spaces, cutting sheet metal, or removing rust in corners, but they lack cutting depth for thicker materials. Cheat sheet: If you own only one grinder, choose 4-½ inch. If you work with thick metal or concrete often, consider a 6-inch with a high-voltage battery system. For automotive bodywork or detail grinding, a compact 3-inch model is a handy second tool.

What safety features matter most?

The most important safety feature is the switch type. A paddle switch that requires constant pressure to run is far safer than a slide switch, especially if you need to drop the tool quickly. Some models add a lock-off button that must be pressed before the paddle can be engaged, preventing accidental startups. Also look for an electronic brake, it stops the disc within seconds of releasing the switch, reducing the risk of cuts from a spinning wheel. Beyond the tool itself, always use the proper guard for cutting or grinding (they are different). Many grinders come with a universal guard that can be rotated to point the spark stream away from you. Tool-free guard adjustment is a real convenience, but not a safety must. Finally, a soft-start feature reduces the jolt when you pull the trigger, helping you maintain control during startup. Never skip personal protective equipment: impact-resistant safety glasses, a face shield, hearing protection, and heavy gloves. A cordless grinder won’t have a cord to trip over, but it will still kick violently if the disc binds, proper PPE is non-negotiable.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a cordless angle grinder to cut tile?

Yes, with the right blade. Use a diamond cutting wheel designed for tile or masonry, and run the grinder at the speed recommended on the blade. Be aware that a cordless grinder may struggle with very hard porcelain tile unless it’s a high-voltage model, wet cutting with a dedicated tile saw is usually easier for large jobs.

How long does the battery last on a typical cordless grinder?

Runtime varies wildly by battery size, disc type, and workload. On a standard 4 or 5 amp-hour battery, expect roughly 15 to 30 minutes of continuous heavy grinding, or more for lighter cutting. Many users buy two batteries and a fast charger so they can swap without downtime.

What’s the difference between a paddle switch and a slide switch?

A paddle switch requires your hand to hold it down while the tool is running; releasing it immediately stops the grinder. A slide switch locks into the on position and must be slid back to turn off. Paddle switches are generally considered safer, especially if you need to drop the tool suddenly, though slide switches can be more comfortable for long, steady cuts.

Do I really need to use the grinding guard?

Yes, always use the guard designed for your current application (cutting or grinding). The guard contains sparks, debris, and broken disc fragments. Removing the guard to fit a larger cut-off wheel or to see better is a leading cause of serious injuries. Use the tool as intended, even pros keep the guard on.

Can I use a cordless angle grinder for rust removal?

Absolutely. Fit a wire cup brush or flap disc, and you can strip rust quickly from metal surfaces. A cordless grinder is especially handy for working on vehicles or outdoor structures where running a cord is inconvenient. Just keep the tool moving to avoid gouging the metal underneath.

How do I maintain my cordless angle grinder?

Keep the air vents clean using compressed air, dust clogs can cause overheating. Inspect the disc for cracks or wear before each use. If it’s a brushed motor, replace the brushes when they are worn to about ¼ inch. For brushless models, there are no brushes to change, but periodic cleaning and checking the battery contacts for debris will extend its life.

In shortA cordless angle grinder is one of the most versatile tools you can own, it cuts, grinds, sands, and preps a wide range of materials. The key is choosing a model that matches your typical workload, not just the highest specs. Stick with your existing battery system, prioritize brushless for regular use, and never compromise on safety features like a spring-return paddle switch and a guard. With the right disc and a couple of charged batteries, you’ll wonder how you ever worked without it.
